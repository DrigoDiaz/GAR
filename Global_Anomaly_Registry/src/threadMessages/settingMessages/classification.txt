<TITLE> Classifications

Every employee must be familiar with the G.U.A.R.D. anomaly classification system. This 
system has three main classification attributes that anomalies can fall under.
<BREAK>

Anomaly Scale:

The first and most broad classification attribute is known as the 'Anomaly Scale.' What
this attribute focuses on is the effective influence range an anomaly has. Below you will see
a list of all possible influence ranges an anomaly can have:

<LIST_TITLE> Possible Anomaly Scaling
<L_I> Individual Level (IL)
<L_I> Regional Level (RL)
<L_I> Global Level (GL)
<BREAK>

Anomaly Typing:

The second classificattion attribute is known as the 'Anomaly Typing.' What this
attribute focuses on is the type of anomaly. Below you will see a list of 
all possible typings an anomaly can have:

<LIST_TITLE> Possible Anomaly Typing
<L_I> Cognitive (COG)
<L_I> Physical (PHY)
<L_I> Temporal (TMP)
<L_I> Spatial (SPA)
<BREAK>

Anomaly Class:

The third and final classification is known as the 'Anomaly Class.' What this
attribute focuses on is the class of the anomaly. Below you will see a list of
all possible classes an anomaly can be:

<LIST_TITLE> Possible Anomaly Class
<L_I> Entity (ENT)
<L_I> Phenomenon (PHEN)
<BREAK>

Example:

An anomaly with the classification of 'IL-COG-ENT,' would be interpetted as
an Individual Level Cogntive Anomaly Entity. This classification helps G.U.A.R.D.
personnel understand the nature and scope of the anomaly to effectively
determine the appropiate procedures to take.
<BREAK>

If additional information is required or a new classification is discovered, please
contact HR or let your assigned mentor know.

<END>
